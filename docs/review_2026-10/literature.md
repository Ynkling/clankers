## Summary

- 12 of 18 references have a reading note. All 12 agree with their note on identifier, title and authors, with one title difference: the note for 2512.24695 reads "*The Illusion of Deep Learning Architecture*", the report "*...Architectures*".
- The two references the report positions itself against most directly, **BDH-CQ** (2608.09888) and **Triadic Linear Attention** (2609.36529), have **no reading note**. BDH-CQ is known in the repository only through "the brief" given to the note-takers (`docs/reading/2510.04871.md:309`, `docs/reading/2502.05171.md:173`). TLA is known only through the audit's search snippet (`docs/audit_2026-10.md:99,102`). The report's claims about both are UNVERIFIABLE from the repository.
- Four one-line uses go beyond what the notes support: the "none of the twelve" sentence (contradicted by the project's own HM-RNN note), the BDH-CQ reconstruction, the recurrent-depth "explains why the frozen memory must enter every step", and Flesch's "a slow integrator must receive only the cue". Two more are loose: the content-routing paper's numbers, and Raven's Gumbel noise "works for a dense read".

## The BDH-CQ reconstruction (tex 222)

What the report says: Pathway's BDH-CQ "describes its memory as 'linear correction rules on S' with linear attention as the special case S_t = S_{t−1} + U_θ(D_t)"; "Our reading (an inference, not a finding): in the taxonomy of [ttr] and [nested], the rules that are affine in S and read S before writing are the delta-rule family (DeltaNet, Longhorn, Gated DeltaNet, DeltaProduct), and the loop matches the recursive-refinement models [trm, recdepth], of which [recdepth] explains why the frozen memory must enter every step. Neither is a distant-cue mechanism."

What the repository supports:

1. **The source was not read.** There is no `docs/reading/2608.09888.md`. Every note that discusses BDH-CQ works from "the brief" (`2510.04871.md:309`, `2502.05171.md:173`). The synthesis quotes §3.2 and §3.3 verbatim (`distant_cues_2026-10.md:51`), but nothing records who extracted those quotes or from which version. The quotations are therefore UNVERIFIABLE here, and the bibliography's nine-author list cannot be checked.
2. **The quoted sentence does not entail the inference.** In the quote, linear attention, which does *not* read S before writing, is called "the conceptually simplest standalone realization of linear correction rules on S". So Pathway's class "linear correction rules" includes the pure additive rule, and plausibly the gated additive ones (row 2 of the synthesis's own table, `distant_cues_2026-10.md:58`). The step from "correction rule" to "reads S before writing" is the synthesis's reading of the word "correction" (`distant_cues_2026-10.md:65`, "read literally ... selects rows 3–5"), and the source sentence cuts against it. The report states only that the delta-rule family consists of the rules that read S before writing, which is true, and leaves the reader to infer that BDH-CQ is in it. The synthesis says "almost certainly" (`distant_cues_2026-10.md:11`), which no evidence in the repository supports.
3. **The loop.** The structural match to TRM and Geiping et al. is reasonable as an analogy (`2510.04871.md:313-319`). "Explains why the frozen memory must enter every step" is not what Geiping et al. explain (row 8 above).
4. **What would resolve it:** a reading note on 2608.09888 written from the paper itself, quoting §3.2–3.3 with page numbers, and a sentence in the report saying the reconstruction rests on one phrase and admits the additive and gated-additive rules.

The report is more careful than the synthesis it summarises ("an inference, not a finding"). The remaining problem is that the inference is presented as following from a taxonomy, when it follows from a reading of one word that the quoted sentence itself contradicts.

## The "twelve papers" sentence (abstract, tex 35; tex 220)

"None of twelve papers reviewed carries an inferred context cue across distance without labels." As shown in row 15, the project's own HM-RNN note says HM-RNN does exactly that, from a dense next-character loss. The report could keep its point by stating the condition that distinguishes its own problem: an inferred cue carried across distance **from a sparse, end-of-sequence loss**. The current wording is an overstatement in the abstract, and it is the claim a reader in this area is most likely to test. Relatedly, two of the papers the report treats as context (TRM, Geiping et al.) are not about routing or context cues at all, which inflates "none of twelve" as evidence.
