## Summary

BDH's Hebbian working memory is split into *k* channels, and a per-token gate distributes each token's write over the channels and blends its read from them. On a synthetic task, *S* streams bind the same keys to different values, cued by a stream token; one channel cannot hold the conflicting bindings at four or eight streams, and a hand-set gate, one channel per stream, can. The question is whether a gate trained only on the task loss finds that partition.

**The main result (Revision 8), unchanged.** For the task's grouped layout, where the cue is within three tokens of every write, a *window gate* (a width-3 causal convolution of the embeddings), slow memory for the first 2400 updates and a plateau-triggered *channel split* that copies the busiest channel's gate row onto the idlest bind eight streams, the streams on disjoint sets of channels, in **38/40** runs of a pre-registered test on two machines with disjoint seeds, against 0/40 for the recurrent-gate recipe (38 vs 0 discordant pairs) and 15/40 without the split (23 vs 0). The recipe uses no stream labels, no restarts and no hinge; the split reads the task's key positions.

**What Revisions 9 and 10 add.** They report what has been done to that result since:

- **A referee.** An independent referee session recomputed the numbers of the report's tables from the records (140 of 156 reproduce; the seven that disagreed were statements, corrected in Revision 8.1), audited the pre-registration of every claim (none edited after a run), compiled the project's claim registry (53 pre-registered tests per machine, of which the headline survives any multiplicity correction), and found statements in Revision 8 that were wrong when committed; they are corrected and listed. It then checked every version of Revision 9's draft the same way, seven passes, and Revision 10's additions ([15](#15-the-referees-report-and-the-claim-registry)).
- **A replication.** A clean-room replication, from the report alone for its first twenty seeds, by a session that had not seen the code when it ran them, bound eight streams 17/20 against 7/20 without the split (10 vs 0), and 18/20 on twenty further seeds after taking the split's probe and key positions from our code (35/40 against 12/40 pooled, 23 vs 0): the model it built agrees with ours to 7×10⁻⁹ in the gates and 7×10⁻⁸ in the logits, the 44 details it logged (34 of them guesses) are stated in [the replication protocol](#what-a-replication-needs-the-protocol-as-this-revision-states-it), and one of them exposed a sampling weakness in the split's own probe ([14](#14-an-independent-replication)).
- **The copy, not the reset — now pre-registered.** On the main line, the second claim of Revision 8 is a pre-registered result on two machines with disjoint seeds: the copy with its reset binds eight streams 18/20 against the reset alone 6/20 (12 vs 0), the reset alone is within the bound of no trigger (6/20 against 8/20, 0 vs 2), and one delta-rule channel binds 0/20 where the recipe binds 18/20 (18 vs 0); the pre-fixed readings "the copy, not the reset" and "the partition is necessary at eight streams against a single delta channel" both apply ([6](#6-the-split-the-copy-the-reset-and-the-trigger), [11](#11-the-memory-rule)).
- **The memory rule, pre-registered (Revision 10).** On screens, the delta-rule write with its write strength fixed rather than learned is not shown worse than the Hebbian write at two, four and eight streams (10/10, 10/10 and 9/10 against 10/10) and binds sooner. Its pre-registered test, `test_delta_recipe`, is complete on both machines: pooled over 40 pairs with disjoint seeds, the delta recipe binds eight streams 35/40 against the Hebbian recipe's 35/40 (*d* = 0; the pre-fixed label "delta not shown worse by more than 2 pairs"), it needs no split to do so (without a trigger 38/40; the split's claim on delta NOT SHOWN, with no room), and the Hebbian write needs the split (35/40 against 17/40, 19 vs 1, *p* = 2×10⁻⁵); the readings "delta: no room; Hebbian: helps" apply, and delta binds sooner on 24 of the 30 seeds both bind. The two machines mirrored each other on the primary comparison (X *d* = −3, L *d* = +3), which is what a pooled claim with disjoint seeds is for. A learned write strength, screened afterwards, collapses to zero and binds 7/10 against the fixed one's 10/10 ([11](#11-the-memory-rule)).
- **A third machine and a second replication phase (Revision 10).** The headline's three eight-stream arms ran on a consumer GPU with CUDA arithmetic on twenty fresh seeds, with the main line's own test code: the recipe 19/20, without the split 6/20, the recurrent gate 0/20 (descriptions, not claims; [5](#5-the-main-result-test_window_gate)). The replication session then reproduced `test_split_copy`'s three arms from its specification on fresh seeds: 18/20, 9/20 and 0/20, each inside the main line's interval by its pre-fixed rule, which separates little at twenty seeds ([14](#14-an-independent-replication)).
- **Distant cues.** On Hebbian memory the layer whose routing matters is the first: perfect gates at the later layers alone bind 0/10. A register that overwrites at the context token, read by a gate at all three layers, carries a cue up to six tokens away 20/20 when nudged by labels, is found label-free on 4/20 seeds (fourteen of the sixteen failures on each memory being key splits, formed in the records before any write at the cue), and is found 20/20 on fresh seeds when a hinge on the gate's key-dependence holds the key split off for the first 2400 updates — a training-time prior that reads the task's key positions and identities, as the split reads key positions ([12](#12-distant-cues)).
- **A dense loss.** Under a dense next-token loss on two interleaved sources a single channel learns to carry the source by itself; at four sources, on a GPU screen that its own validity rule leaves untested (the perfect gate ended at 0.880 and 0.892 against a bar of 0.9), neither one channel nor either learned gate did, on ten seeds each ([19](#19-distance-to-a-language-model)).
- **Caveats.** The three main-line specifications the sessions drafted next (a hinged register at a distance, the key term at two streams, sixteen keys at *D* = 128) each failed review or their own validity screen as written, so the next pre-registered test has a specification to revise before it has a run ([15](#15-the-referees-report-and-the-claim-registry)). Two-stream key splits resist repair and are prevented by the hinge's key term on a screen (20/20 against 15/20), as the register's are; a replication's probe can be wrong about a plateau; the title is scoped to what was shown. Exploratory screens are not claims; nothing from them is a result until a main-line test repeats it.
